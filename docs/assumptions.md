# Assumptions

Every guess made in the absence of field data. Walk this list with the pilot vendor at the end of the build, item by item.

Mark the corresponding code with `// ASSUMPTION: ... See docs/assumptions.md#anchor`.

| # | Anchor | Assumption | Why we're guessing | How we'd know | Cost if wrong |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `#hold-ttl` | Soft holds ("pencils") default to a 24h TTL, extendable by the desk, with a challenge call when someone else wants to confirm. **Shipped in 0022**: `create_booking` stamps `pencil_expires_at = now() + 24h`, overridable per call and per org (`settings.pencil_ttl_hours`); expiry is a read predicate plus opportunistic pruning inside the booking RPCs, never a cron. | The research could not find a primary source documenting the pencil/challenge convention on the open web, despite it being central to the booking design. | Ask the vendor how a hold is placed today, who calls whom, and how long it lives. | Medium. The state machine is right; only the TTL and the escalation trigger would change — both are settings, not schema. |
| 2 | `#deposit-norms` | Deposits are collected per booking, in cash or as a held cheque, and are refunded only after check-in reaches `inspected_clear`. | No sourceable data on Pakistani rental-house deposit practice. | Ask what they take, from whom, and what they've done when gear came back damaged. | High. The deposit state machine and the refund gate are built around this. |
| 3 | `#cash-split` | Money is cash-first, with running client credit (*udhaar*) as a real instrument. Card is rare. | Pakistan's 88% digital retail transaction share is formal-rails data and does not evidence that a rental house takes card. | Ask how the last ten jobs were paid. | Medium. Affects which payment surfaces get built first, not the ledger design. |
| 4 | `#coi` | Formal Certificates of Insurance are uncommon here; CNIC + guarantor + held cheque are the trust artefacts instead. `customer_credentials` generalises COI to cover all four. | Research found the COI norm documented only for US rental houses. | Ask what they require before handing over a Rs 4,000,000 camera to someone new. | Low. The credentials table already generalises; only the default kinds change. |
| 5 | `#week-rate` | A week bills as 3 day-rates (`week_equals_days = 3`), configurable per org. | The 3-day-week convention is sourced (MovieMaker) but for US houses. | Ask for their current rate card and how they quote a 10-day job. | High if the default is wrong on day one, but it is one org setting. |
| 6 | `#weekend` | The weekend day-mask is Sat/Sun, with Friday treated as a full day. | Friday Jumma is a real half-day in Pakistani production scheduling, but whether it affects *billing* is unknown. | Ask how they bill a Thursday-to-Saturday job. | Low. The calendar object takes a day-mask; this is data, not code. |
| 7 | `#fleet-size` | A typical Lahore house holds ~500–4,000 taggable assets, sized as an 8–20MB device working set. | No sourceable fleet-size data for this market. | Count their shelves. | High if wildly off — it is the basis for choosing cursor-pull sync over PowerSync. Above ~200MB, revisit. |
| 8 | `#roman-urdu` | Roman Urdu (Latin script) is more usable than Nastaliq for warehouse staff, and avoids bidi problems next to LTR asset codes. | Reasoned from WhatsApp typing habits, not tested with actual warehouse staff. | Show two screens to three techs. | Medium. Changes the font package and every fixed-height row token. |
| 9 | `#literacy` | Prep techs can read short Roman-Urdu labels but the scanner should be operable primarily by icon, colour and haptic. | The research prescribes low-literacy tolerance without quantifying it. | Watch a tech use it without explanation. | High for adoption, low for architecture. |
| 10 | `#shared-device` | Warehouse phones are shared between 2–4 staff, hence PIN-based fast user switching rather than per-user OTP. | Inferred from the business shape and from Current RMS's documented OTP failures. | Ask how many phones exist and who carries them. | Medium. The auth model is built for the harder case, so being wrong is safe. |
| 11 | `#tag-survival` | Printed QR labels survive gaffer tape residue, road cases and Lahore summer heat well enough that re-tagging is occasional, not routine. | Untested. The whole design assumes tags mostly persist. | Tag one rack and check it after a month of real jobs. | High. If tags fail often, the manual fallback stops being a fallback and becomes the primary path. |
| 12 | `#qc-gate` | Check-in reconciliation is **advisory** — it alerts, it never blocks closing a return. | A judgement call. A house might prefer it to block. | Watch one real return at 7pm. | Low. One flag. |
| 13 | `#clock-skew` | The 5-minute threshold for back-correcting device clock drift. | An explicit guess in the architecture document. | Instrument `clock_offset_ms` and look at the real distribution across the first 20 devices. | Low. Ordering is by `server_time`; this only affects display and late-arrival detection. |
| 14 | `#value-threshold` | The "new customer + high value" fraud gate triggers above a configurable rupee threshold. | No data on what counts as high-value here. | Ask what they'd want a second opinion on. | Low. It's an org setting. |
| 15 | `#demo-rates` | The demo's per-product day rates and replacement values (FX9 ~Rs 25,000/day and ~Rs 3.5M replacement, lenses Rs 8–12k/day, an XLR Rs 300), and western digit grouping (Rs 250,000, never lakh-style 2,50,000) on every money label. | No sourceable Lahore rate card; the grouping convention is reasoned from local invoices and bank statements, not verified with a vendor. | Ask for their actual rate card and one paper challan, and copy both. | Low. The rates are demo seed data the real card replaces; the grouping is one formatter (`formatRupees`) in `packages/core`. |
| 16 | `#buffer-defaults` | Prep and turnaround buffers default to 2h before and 4h after the customer window (`settings.prep_buffer_hours` / `turnaround_buffer_hours`, per org; shortenable per booking at confirm — PLAN override 7). | The architecture's illustrative numbers ("a 2-hour prep window and a 4-hour turnaround") promoted to defaults; no field data on real Lahore turnaround practice. | Watch one same-day turnaround: when does gear actually become re-rentable after a 7pm return? | Low. Two org settings and a per-booking override already exist; only the defaults would change. |
| 17 | `#booking-exposure` | The credential gate measures a booking's exposure as the summed **replacement value** of what it promises (`confirm_booking`, 0022 D9). | Rental rates don't exist until phase 3, and replacement value is what actually walks out the door — but a vendor might reason in rental value or deposit size instead. | Ask what number they look at before releasing gear to a stranger. | Low. One SUM expression inside one RPC. |
| 18 | `#least-utilized` | "Least-utilised" allocation picks the unit with the lowest `rental_days_since_service`, then lowest `cycle_count` (0022 D4). | Days-since-service is the only usage meter that exists (0021); true lifetime utilisation isn't tracked yet, and a house might prefer round-robin or newest-first. | Ask which body they'd hand out first and why. | Low. One ORDER BY inside confirm_booking; the constraint re-checks whatever the pick is. |
| 19 | `#weekend-free` | Days in the rate card's `weekend_mask` are **not billed** — but the mask defaults to **empty: every day bills**. A house that gives the weekend away sets `{6,7}` (Sat/Sun per #6, Friday a full day) on its card; then a Fri 10:00 → Mon 10:00 job counts one day and a weekend-only job still bills `min_billable_days`. **Shipped in 0024** (D3): the mask is per card, the trace lists every dropped date. | The "weekend = one day" rule is a sourced US convention; Lahore's highest-value jobs are shaadi weekends, so a default that quietly gave them away would be an under-bill no client ever reports. Opt-in was judged the honest default. | Ask how they bill a Thursday-to-Saturday job, and whether any client is ever excused a Sunday the gear sat idle. | Low. One array per card, no code. |
| 20 | `#seasonal-pricing` | Dec–Feb is the wedding season and it is **shading only**: `ensure_default_calendar` seeds the rows at `rate_multiplier = 1.0`; holidays (Eid) are rows the desk adds with whatever multiplier they use. **Shipped in 0024** (D6/D7): the pipeline applies the MAX multiplier of any calendar day inside the job, booking-wide. | Vendor-dream-plan flags "is seasonal/peak pricing actually practiced, or is pricing purely relational discounting?" as unanswered. A 1.0 default cannot surcharge anyone by accident. | Ask whether an Eid-week or December quote is ever higher than a March one for the same kit, and by how much. | Low. Edit the rows; nothing in code changes. Whether MAX (vs per-day blend) is the right rule is a follow-up question for the same afternoon. |
| 21 | `#rental-day` | A rental day is **24 hours from pickup**, dated in the org timezone: out Tue 10:00, back Fri 10:00 is three days; back at 10:01 is four. **Shipped in 0024** (D2). | The alternative — every calendar date touched — bills the return morning, which a return-by-the-same-time house would not defend; but a grace period (return by noon = same day) is common and unverified here. | Ask what time gear has to be back to avoid another day, and whether there is a grace window. | Medium. `ceil(duration / 24h)` is one expression; a grace window is one more knob on the card. |
| 22 | `#override-final` | A manual rate override is the **final** per-day rate: the calendar multiplier does not apply on top of it. **Shipped in 0024** (D8). | "Never fight the owner's judgement" (research) — when the owner says Rs 20,000 for this client, that is the number, not Rs 20,000 × 1.25. A house that thinks of overrides as "base rate before season" would disagree. | Show the owner an Eid-week quote with an override and ask what the line should read. | Low. One `case` in `price_booking` step 5; the trace already reports `multiplier_applied`. |
| 23 | `#sub-hire-cost` | The cost side of margin-before-quote is **every live expense** tagged to the booking (or to the job it became), whatever its kind — transport to set counts as much as the sub-hired lens. **Shipped in 0024** (D9). | The vendor-dream-plan says "sub-rental costs and discounts net out on the quote screen"; whether the house wants transport/consumables in the same number is a guess. | Ask what they subtract before deciding a quote is worth it. | Low. One `where` clause in `booking_sub_hire_cost`; the kinds are already on every row. |
| 24 | `#wedding-season` | December–February is wedding season in Lahore: the calendar shades those months and the desk expects them booked 4–6 months out (`seasonFor` in `packages/core/src/bookings.ts`; vendor-dream-plan Phase C1). | The plan's own line ("Dec–Feb is booked 4–6 months out") is the only source; no vendor has confirmed the months or whether Ramadan/Eid shifts them year to year. | Ask which months they refuse pencils for and start asking for deposits early; look at last year's calendar. | Low. One pure function of the month; the shading is cosmetic and the booking rules do not branch on it. |
| 25 | `#escalation-ladder` | The overdue ladder is day 1 WhatsApp nudge → day 3 call → day 7 late-fee draft → day 14 manager escalation with a blacklist consideration (`escalationStep` in `packages/core/src/bookings.ts`). PLAN override 19 sketches "1 day → tech, 3 days → manager WhatsApp, 14 days → a missing claim"; this is the desk-facing reading, with the day-7 money rung the khata already drafts. | No field data on when a Lahore house stops nudging and starts calling, or when it writes a client off. | Ask what they did on the last three late returns, day by day. | Low. Four rungs in one array; each carries an action kind a screen maps to one button, so re-ordering is data. |
| 26 | `#local-credential-flag` | On the phone, the confirm gate reads one local flag (`customers.credentials_verified`) in place of the server's derived `verified_customers` view, and the refusal says so (`gate: 'local_flag'` in `confirmBooking`, `apps/app/src/demo/bookings.ts`). | The phone carries no credential rows (0009/0015 keep CNICs off it), so "verified" cannot be derived locally; the server re-runs its own view when the op replays. | Watch whether a desk ever confirms offline for a stranger the server later refuses — that count is the cost of the proxy. | Low. The server's gate is the law; the flag only decides what the desk sees before the pipe drains. |
